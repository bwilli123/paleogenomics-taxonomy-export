Sümer et al. (2024, *Nature*): high-coverage whole-genome sequencing (24x Ranis13, 20x Zlatý kůň) using single-stranded library preparation (Meyer) to extract highly degraded ancient DNA from bone fragments otherwise lost to history.

The Zlatý kůň/Ranis population represents the deepest known split from the "Out-of-Africa" lineage ever sampled — yet shows total population discontinuity: zero genetic contribution to present-day non-Africans. A biologically deepest-divergent branch that was nonetheless a genetic dead end.

Stratigraphic continuity confirmed across 1930s and 2010s excavations (Ranis 12) linking fragmented archaeological contexts via molecular precision.

Effective population size of only ~160–300 breeding individuals: high recent consanguinity (long Runs of Homozygosity, HLA-loci homozygosity at 3 of 5 loci in Ranis13 — a measurable loss of pathogen-resistance capacity from inbreeding) coexisting with high *overall* heterozygosity matching other non-Africans, resolved via PSMC modeling: the group branched from a much larger parent population during the primary Out-of-Africa bottleneck and migrated into Europe too recently for ancestral heterozygosity to erode despite small immediate group size.

Identity-by-Descent (IBD) sharing confirmed a Ranis 4–6 mother-daughter pair and a 5th/6th-degree kinship between Zlatý kůň and Ranis 12 across 230km — direct evidence these sites were inhabited by the same small, mobile breeding population.
