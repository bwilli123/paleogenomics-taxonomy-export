Because all populations carrying the Neanderthal pulse shared by Ranis/Zlatý kůň also carry Denisovan ancestry, the Denisovan admixture event must likewise post-date 45–49ka — establishing a hard genomic "floor" for any successful non-African lineage.

**The Australian Paradox:** archaeological dating places human settlement of Australia (Madjedbebe) at ~60,000 years ago — apparently predating the 45–49ka genomic floor. Since all confirmed-successful lineages carry the shared Neanderthal pulse, ancestors already in Australia at 60ka could not possess this marker.

**Resolution via the "Fourth Nation" displacement theory:** a "First Nation" (archaic or modern humans present at 60ka, predating the pulse) was displaced by a later "Fourth Nation" pulse carrying the 45–49ka Neanderthal signature — meaning the 60ka Australian artifacts may have been produced by a population that was subsequently genetically replaced and left no living descendants, rather than by ancestors of present-day Aboriginal Australians directly.

Any modern human remains found outside Africa older than 50,000 years are, by this framework, a priori excluded from the successful non-African lineage — they represent earlier, failed dispersals.

See also: [[paleogenomics/neanderthal-admixture-dating|Dating the Shared Neanderthal Admixture Pulse]] — the Ranis/Zlatý kůň lineage is itself an example of a failed, non-contributing dispersal.
