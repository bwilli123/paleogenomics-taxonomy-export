Sümer (corresponding author) established the "80 generations" metric: Neanderthal admixture is constrained to approximately 80 generations before the Ranis individuals lived, assuming a 29-year generation interval.

This places the shared Neanderthal introgression pulse at 45,000–49,000 years ago (cal BP), anchored by radiocarbon dating of RNI013 (43.4–46.6 ka).

A continued-admixture model (Iasi et al. 2021) fits the data better than a single-pulse model — admixture duration affects the length of inherited genomic segments through generations of recombination.

Neanderthal ancestry segments measure 2.8–3.0% in these genomes; "Neanderthal deserts" (chromosomal regions devoid of archaic ancestry from strong negative selection) were mostly established within 80 generations — with a notable persistent exception on Chromosome 1 in the Zlatý kůň genome that survived the selection sweep clearing archaic ancestry elsewhere.
